Skipii — a real Settings page

Today every setting is a card in the account sheet: Language, Appearance, Player controls, Captions, Shorts, SponsorBlock. That works for six. It won't for thirty. Below: what Ledgii's settings page does, the categories I'd use, and four directions. The phones are live: tap categories, flip switches, pick a theme. They all share one set of settings, so a change in one shows in the others.

The categories (same in all four)

Each one is useful today, even with just one or two settings, and there's room to add more later (in grey).

  • Playback: player controls (pill / on the video), remember speed, audio only. Later: default quality, autoplay, double-tap seek length, hold-for speed, background play.
  • Captions: show, look, size, phone-translated languages. Later: default language, transcript options.
  • SponsorBlock: on/off, the eight segments, the credit. Later: skip notice, minimum length.
  • Shorts: show the tab. Later: how many to keep offline, loop, the glass bar.
  • Appearance: mode, theme. Later: thumbnail size, card density.
  • Language: app language.
  • Notifications: the belled channels in one list. Later: quiet hours, a daily digest.
  • Account: sign in/out, the full-sync warning. Later: what syncs.
  • About: version and update check, What's new, GitHub, credits. Later: Storage (search history, Shorts bank, cache) can become its own category.

BBento

Ledgii's compact tile in a two-column grid. Each tile shows its own state: Playback draws the controls you picked, Captions shows its look, SponsorBlock its segment colours. Quick settings work on the tile itself (Shorts' switch, the theme dots). A category opens out of its tile.

CGrouped, with search

Android 16's own Settings look: a large title, a search field that finds any setting in any category, and connected rows grouped under labels (Watching, Look & language…). Try typing "speed", "dark" or "sponsor" in the left phone.

DTabs in the account sheet

Keeps your 2026-10-04 choice that settings live in the account sheet. The sheet opens to full height: the account sits on one line at the top, the categories are a row of tabs, and the content slides sideways between them. Everything is one level deep.

My lean: A, plus C's search field under the title. You get Ledgii's look and summaries, and search takes over once the front page fills up. B is the fun pick if you want Settings to feel as playful as the player bar.
