The player's bar, within thumb reach

Five ways to make the open player's −10 · play · +10 bar a bit bigger and a bit higher (today: 216×62 dp, play 96×50, bottom ≈35 dp above the screen edge). The tab bar itself never moves — only the player's bar rises, so each direction also has its own tabs → player bar morph. Tap the mini-player to open, drag the video down to close (the bar scrubs with your finger), or drag the slider under a phone to scrub the morph frame by frame.

What stays the same

  • The tab bar (328×62, same spot) and the mini-player don't move. Signed out or in, nothing about Home changes.
  • The play pill is still the progress bar (pale track, full-primary fill, glyph two-toned across the edge) — except C, where it becomes a ring.
  • Everything is still driven by morph.progress in layer/draw lambdas only, so a swipe down scrubs it back into tabs under the finger.
  • ±10 bursts, comments-panel step-down, fullscreen/PiP/sheets covering it — unchanged.

Sizes at a glance

  • 0.5.28 — bar 216×62, play 96×50, ±10 50, bottom 35.
  • A Lift — bar 264×72, play 120×60, ±10 60, bottom 71 (+36).
  • B Float — play 136×68, ±10 circles 64, centres 114 apart, bottom 80 (+45).
  • C Arc — play circle 84, ±10 circles 62 dipping 26 dp, play bottom 76.
  • D Dock — full-width 156 dp dock, play 140×64, ±10 64, row 104 dp up; scrubber + times below.
  • E Handed — bar 252×70, play 112×58, bottom 88, shifted 64 dp toward the thumb.

Code impact

  • A is the smallest change: new PlaySlotWidth/SeekSlotWidth/height and a lift translationY ramp in NavPill.
  • B and C draw the bar as separate shapes; B's merge/split needs a metaball path (or a stretched connector) instead of an SVG filter.
  • D grows the bar into a surface; bottomClearance rises to ~160 dp, and the scrubber row is new.
  • E needs a Left/Right setting (account sheet) plus the long-press flip.
  • All five raise the player's bottomClearance so the last "More videos" card clears the bar.

Thumb zone overlay

A rough guide only: a right thumb on a Pixel held one-handed pivots near the right edge about three-quarters down; green = easy, amber = stretch. It's there to compare heights between directions, not as measured data.
