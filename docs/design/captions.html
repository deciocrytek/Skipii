Is it fully possible?

Works today probed 2026-11-01

  • The track list comes free with the player answer Skipii already gets: a test video listed 6 tracks — English, English (auto-generated), German, Japanese, Portuguese (Brazil), Spanish (Latin America).
  • The caption files download with no sign-in and no PO token (HTTP 200, unlike streams). Formats: YouTube's own srv3 XML and WebVTT. Links are signed and last ~1 day, so they're fetched with the video and cached.
  • Auto-generated tracks carry per-word timing (<s t="559"> strangers</s>), which is exactly what YouTube's word-by-word roll-up uses.

Ours to build no blockers

  • A small srv3 parser (cues, words, sound tags, the creator's positions) and a Compose caption layer over the one video surface, on the same AV-synced clock.
  • Media3's own subtitle renderer isn't used: it can't roll up per word, lift out of the controls' way or draw Skipii's look. It is used for Android's caption settings (CaptioningManager).
  • Transcript panel, account card, bar tool, fullscreen card, PiP. Captions in Shorts can come later from the same data.

Not like YouTube one gap

  • YouTube's own auto-translate is refused. Asking for a translated track (tlang=fr, tlang=de) got HTTP 429 "Sorry…" both times, while the untranslated track loaded fine right after. It's the same wall as PO tokens / the player JS (0.5.49).
  • Instead: translated on the phone (Google's ML Kit, ~30 MB per language, downloaded once, offline after, 50+ languages). Good, a step below YouTube's (it translates line by line, YouTube translates the whole text).
  • To check on Derek's 5G: when the bot check refuses the player answer, the track list has to come from the VISIONOS fallback.
